## Radiographs

**Adapted from:** Mikael Häggström, *Normal posteroanterior (PA) chest radiograph (X-ray).jpg* and *Normal lateral
chest radiograph (X-ray).jpg*, Wikimedia Commons, 28 June 2017. Both images are released under

### Changes made

1. Both films were converted to greyscale and resized ×0.6. The pixel spacing is **estimated** at 0.25 mm from
   typical thoracic dimensions; the originals carry no calibration.
2. The burnt-in "Dx" / "Sin" side labels on the PA were removed by inpainting, with grain added back, because the
   simulator places its own lead marker.
3. Each film was extended by 8 cm on every side with a progressively blurred edge-replicate. This **synthetic**
   margin is only seen if you collimate wider than the original film.
4. In the browser, every image you make is the real film:
   - cropped to your light field;
   - magnified for SID and for AP / right-lateral set-ups;
   - warped for expiration;
   - overlaid with **simulated** scapulae, arms, jaw, necklace, bra hooks / underwire and hair;
   - blurred for breathing;
   - re-contrasted / noised for your kV, grid and exposure;
   - stamped with your lead marker.

   None of these effects is a real finding.
5. Exposure index, deviation index and DAP come from a simplified teaching model, not a calibrated system.
6. The 3-D room and patient are generic and procedural.

Educational simulation. It isn't a medical device and isn't for clinical use.
